# Sentence-by-sentence breakdowns of two analyzed scripts

These breakdowns paraphrase each sentence in neutral wording and label its job. They exist to calibrate rhythm, density, and structure. Do not reuse their phrasing in new scripts. Both source transcripts were auto-generated captions, so some figures were garbled; treat numbers below as indicative and verify any figure from a real source before using it.

- Job codes

## Job codes
HOOK, WHY (motive), STAKES, MECH (how it works), TURN, ESC (escalation), NUM (hard number or date), IRONY, HUMAN (small human detail), ARCH (archive clip), TIME (time jump), CODA.

## What repeats across both

1. The first sentence carries the outcome.
2. The hinge is announced (a bare hook or a "didn't last long" line).
3. Expectation, then violation, both times.
4. Mechanism sentences make the failure believable.
5. Numbers do the arguing; the best effect is a contrast (promised versus paid, planned versus actual).
6. Tiny human details make scale real.
7. Time is compressed or slowed deliberately.
8. The opposing side gets one fair sentence.
9. Anticlimax is allowed when evidence runs out.
10. Endings are institutional, not moral, and no call to action appears in either transcript.

Approximate stats: about 14 to 16 words per sentence on average; about one sentence in four carries a number or date; roughly 150 spoken words per minute.
