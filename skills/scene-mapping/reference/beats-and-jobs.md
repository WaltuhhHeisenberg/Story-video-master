# Beats and the visual's job

## Segmenting into beats

Do not split by sentence or by a fixed time. Start a new beat when any of these changes:

1. **Subject**: who or what the narration is about.
2. **Place**: where we are.
3. **Time**: when we are.
4. **Question**: what the viewer is now wondering.

A beat is one idea, usually 5 to 15 seconds. A very short beat (under 4 seconds) is fine for a punchline or a hard fact. A beat over 20 seconds should be re-checked: it probably holds two ideas.

Shots are the smaller units inside a beat. Beat boundaries are natural places for designed transitions. Shot boundaries inside a beat are almost always hard cuts or motion carries.

## The visual's job

For each beat, ask what the picture needs to do. The answer picks the scene type.

| Job | Question it answers | Usual scene types |
|---|---|---|
| `show_event` | Something happens. Show it. | character_action |
| `show_place` | Where are we and what is it like? | environment |
| `show_time` | When are we, or how much time passed? | environment with changed light, kinetic_text, comparison_graphic |
| `show_mechanism` | How does it work? Viewer cannot picture it. | mechanism |
| `show_relationship` | A vs B, before vs after, cause and effect. | comparison_graphic, split environment |
| `show_stakes` | What is at risk, so the viewer cares? | character_action close, object_closeup |
| `hold_question` | Keep an open loop alive without answering it. | object_closeup, environment held, black |
| `introduce_character` | Who is this? | object_closeup (belongings first), character_action |

Mapping goes wrong when the scene type is chosen first because it looks good. Choose the job, then the type.

## Story function

Describes the beat's role in the video's shape, not what is drawn.

- `hook`: the opening claim, image, or question. Must land inside the first 30 seconds.
- `setup`: context the viewer needs before the story can move.
- `escalation`: stakes or complications rise.
- `reveal`: the answer or turn. Needs a beat of black or silence before it, or a held image, so it lands.
- `explanation`: how or why something works.
- `bridge`: links two sections, often carries a transition.
- `rehook`: a new question or surprising fact placed to restart attention, about every 60 seconds.
- `resolution`: closes a major loop.

## Handling abstract lines

When the narration is abstract ("the establishment resisted new ideas"), convert it to something drawable, in this order of preference:

1. A concrete instance (a committee refusing a paper at a table).
2. A mechanism (a gate that stays shut as papers pile up in front of it).
3. A specific metaphor. A generic lightbulb or handshake is filler; reject it.
4. Kinetic text, if the idea is a claim and not a scene.

Record in `assumptions` that the concrete instance is an invented illustration, if the script did not mention it.
